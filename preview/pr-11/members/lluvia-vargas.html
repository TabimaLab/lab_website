Lluvia Vargas

Research Associate

Lluvia earned her degree in Biotechnology Engineering from the Instituto Tecnológico de Sonora, and a M.Sc. and Ph.D. in Life Sciences in the Department of Microbiology at the Centro de Investigación Científica y de Educación Superior de Ensenada (CICESE).

Lluvia’s esearch has focused on fungi in environments that remain underexplored because they are hostile and/or extreme. Her master’s project characterized the fungal diversity of different soil microhabitats in a semiarid ecosystem that is an endemic area for the pathogenic fungus Coccidioides sp. For her doctoral project, she described the composition and distribution patterns of fungal communities in deep-sea sediments of the Gulf of Mexico, elucidating the abiotic factors that influence their diversity.

Currently, Lluvia is a Research Associate at Oregon State University and Clark University, study fungal genomics, focusing on Basidiobolus, a fungus found in the gut of reptiles. My current work is part of two multidisciplinary NSF projects focused on 1) understanding the dynamics of the herpetofauna (reptiles and amphibians) gut microbiome and 2) the evolutionary and population dynamics of secondary metabolism.
