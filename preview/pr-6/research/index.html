Research

We combine evolutionary theory, genomics, computational biology and mycology to learn how fungal species and populations evolve, and we build tools to identify them.

Life cycle plasticity and genome evolution in Basidiobolus

We study how Basidiobolus moves among vertebrate guts, leaf litter, soil, insects and aquatic sediments. With whole-genome sequencing, comparative genomics and secondary metabolite profiling, we test whether specialists and generalists differ in genome architecture and in their chemical defenses.

Microbiomes and host–fungal interactions in amphibians

We sample skin and gut fungal communities across amphibian species to learn how host ecology, urbanization and infection pressure shape microbial assembly. Field studies in New England and the Pacific Northwest are paired with culture-based experiments that uncover new fungal diversity.

Antifungal resistance in emerging Zoopagomycota

We examine intrinsic and induced resistance to common antifungals, including how biosynthetic gene clusters are regulated and which molecular defenses switch on under drug challenge. We combine transcriptomics, metabolomics and functional assays, in collaboration with partners at UW–Madison.

Urbanization and waterway microbial ecology

We investigate how land use shapes microbial diversity in sediment, water and wildlife along the Tatnuck Brook watershed and other regional systems. High-throughput sequencing and ecological modeling help us identify what drives fungal community change, with uses for environmental monitoring.
